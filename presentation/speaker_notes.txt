SLIDE 1
Opening (30 seconds): We are Team Outliers. Our project studies how limited quantum measurements affect a classical regression model, and whether measurement uncertainty can guide regularization. We built a fixed two-qubit simulator and compared four readout methods. The main result is mixed: the covariance-aware method does not consistently improve on Ridge. We will explain the circuit, evaluation and evidence.

SLIDE 2
About 50 seconds. A shot means preparing and measuring a circuit once. Repeating it estimates an expectation value. With few shots, that feature estimate fluctuates, even for a perfect simulated circuit. We keep the quantum circuit fixed and train a classical linear predictor. We ask whether estimating the uncertainty of those features helps regularize that predictor. We compare against Ridge at the same shot budgets. Literature: Kreplin and Roth, Quantum 8, 1385 (2024), DOI 10.22331/q-2024-06-25-1385; Hu et al., Phys. Rev. X 13, 041020 (2023).

SLIDE 3
About 60 seconds. The circuit has two qubits initially in zero. Rotation gates encode x, and CNOT and CZ introduce interactions. We do not optimize any gate angles. We measure both qubits in three independently acquired settings: X, Y and Z. Each setting yields the two individual means and their product, so there are nine features. Shots refer to each setting, so 32 means 96 circuit executions per input. We fit two synthetic target functions, drawing x uniformly from minus pi to pi. Twenty fresh datasets each have five acquisitions. Each dataset has 64 training, 32 validation and 128 test inputs. This is an ideal CPU simulation; no hardware noise or GPU is involved. Source: src/qml_project/quantum.py; configs/measurement_extension.json; docs/EXTENSION_PROTOCOL.md.

SLIDE 4
About 60 seconds. All four models use exactly the same measured features. OLS fits the data without a penalty. Ridge discourages large readout weights. DiagonalCov adds a penalty based on the variance of each measured feature. FullCov additionally keeps correlations between features measured in the same setting. C is measurement covariance at fixed inputs, not feature variation across the dataset. It is estimated from training counts only and transformed using training feature scales. The intercept is not penalized. Both covariance methods already include Ridge; gamma zero turns the covariance term off. Each regularized method receives 32 validation candidates. The grids have different dimensions, so this is matched candidate count, not identical search complexity. The penalty is an adaptation of existing work, especially Baumann et al., arXiv:2604.28160v2 (2026), not a new general algorithm. Sources: src/measurement_extension/covariance.py and models.py.

SLIDE 5
About 60 seconds. Lower test mean squared error is better. The left panel is sine and the right panel is mixed harmonics; their vertical ranges differ. Each plotted value averages five acquisitions per dataset, then twenty dataset means. For readability these charts show means only; the report provides sample standard deviations. They are not significance tests. All methods improve as shots increase in these tested conditions. Their curves almost coincide: the shot-budget effect is much larger than the method difference. FullCov has lower primary mean MSE than Ridge in just one of the six target and budget settings. Source: results/measurement_extension/summary.json, primary clean-label rows, shots 32/128/512.

SLIDE 6
About 60 seconds. This chart zooms in on the small difference between FullCov and Ridge. Positive percentages mean improvement. Only mixed harmonics at 512 shots improves, by about 0.71 percent; the other five primary means are slightly worse. The diagonal/full ablation is also mixed, with FullCov better in three of six conditions. FullCov selected zero covariance strength in 43 to 57 percent of primary fits. Our outcome is a reproducible implementation and a controlled negative or mixed empirical result. We are not claiming a new regularizer, statistical significance, general superiority or quantum advantage. The separate noisy-label sensitivity analysis gives four of six improvements, but does not replace the primary result. A future hypothesis is covariance shrinkage; it needs a new protocol and fresh held-out evaluation. Source: results/measurement_extension/summary.json, paired_comparisons.csv, docs/EXTENSION_RESULTS.md. Methodological precedent: Baumann et al., arXiv:2604.28160v2.
